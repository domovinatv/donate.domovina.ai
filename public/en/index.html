Custody architecture

      
On-chain donations rest on two layers of trust:


      

        

          
1. Monerium EMI

          

            Icelandic regulated e-money institution (licensed by FME, MiCA-compliant since 2024).
            Issues EURe — a euro stablecoin where every token is 102% backed
            by short-term sovereign/corporate bonds and segregated bank deposits.
            Reserves are legally separated from the company's own funds — in case of insolvency
            they go directly to EURe holders, not into the insolvency estate.
          

          monerium.com/eure →
        

        

          
2. DOMOVINA Safe

          

            Safe (2-of-3) multisig controlled by the DOMOVINA team — no
            payout or conversion to IBAN can be executed without at least two independent
            signatures. Hardware wallets and an expanded signer set are planned for the
            next phase.
          

          Safe UI →
        

      


      
What this means in practice

      

        As with money in a bank, trust here rests on regulator and license,
        not on standalone smart-contract mathematics. As the issuer, Monerium retains admin
        powers (mint, burn, address blacklist) — and this is a feature, not a flaw.
        In 2022 these powers were used to
        recover 1.5M EURe stolen from the LCX exchange
        and reissue them to the legitimate holders. The same powers can in principle be
        applied to any address — which is why it matters to know and disclose who stands
        behind the stablecoin we use.
      

      

        SEPA donation flow: your bank transfer → Monerium IBAN → Monerium mints EURe on
        Gnosis Chain → EURe lands in the DOMOVINA Safe. Payout flow: 2 of 3 signers sign
        a redemption → Monerium burns EURe → SEPA outbound to the end recipient (e.g. an
        infrastructure vendor).
